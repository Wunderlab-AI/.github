# Code of Conduct

Wunderlab is a small project that wants to stay a friendly place to build, learn, and ask questions, including for people who are just starting out. This code applies in every Wunderlab space: repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, and anywhere else someone represents the project.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and patient.** Many people here are still learning. Questions are welcome, and nobody should be mocked for not knowing something.
- **Assume good faith.** Disagree about ideas without attacking people.
- **Give and take feedback well.** Make reviews specific and constructive, and receive them graciously.
- **Respect privacy and boundaries,** including how someone wants to be credited, or whether they want to be named at all.
- **Credit other people's work.**

## What we don't accept

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments, including about someone's skill level, background, identity, or appearance.
- Sexualized language or attention, intimidation, or stalking.
- Sharing someone's private information, such as their real name, location, or contact details, without their permission.
- Sustained disruption, spam, or arguing in bad faith.
- Other conduct that would reasonably be seen as inappropriate in a professional community.

## Reporting

Email **conduct@wunderlab.eu**. Reports are read only by the maintainers and are handled privately. We won't share who reported something without their consent, except where needed to deal with it. We aim to acknowledge reports within 7 days.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, issues, and pull requests, lock threads, and warn, temporarily restrict, or permanently ban someone from the project. We aim to be proportionate: a first, minor problem usually gets a private note, while serious or repeated behavior gets stronger action. If you think a decision was wrong, reply to the email and a maintainer will take another look.

## Attribution
